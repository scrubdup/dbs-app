# PIKK Creator Rights, Authenticity & Impersonation Policy — Draft v0.1
Status: policy proposal, subject to review by qualified U.S. platform/IP counsel. Not a promise of immunity or a statement that reporting/rights-enforcement tooling is already operational.

## 1. Scope and core rule
Applies to posts, profiles, music, AI artists, livestreams, video, artwork, merchandise, marketplace listings, ads, names and branding. Creators must hold the rights and permissions needed for every use, including distribution, commercialization, sampling, synchronization, sound recordings, compositions, trademarks, likenesses and identifiable voices. Some authorized uses may be covered by fair use or other legal exceptions; PIKK must permit review rather than assume every claim is infringement.

## 2. Prohibited conduct — examples
1. Uploading, reselling, or distributing another person's music, beats, samples, compositions, recordings, videos, photos, art or writing without rights or a valid legal exception.
2. Selling counterfeits, unauthorized replicas, pirated downloads, copied artwork, designs, merchandise or listings falsely claiming to be official.
3. Misusing artist, creator, designer, brand, label or business names, handles, trademarks, logos or trade dress to suggest a false affiliation, sponsorship or endorsement.
4. Creating deceptive impersonation profiles, stores, fan pages, impersonated artist releases or false 'verified' identity claims.
5. Using AI voice clones, face swaps, digital replicas or someone's recognizable name, image or likeness without the required consent, particularly for monetization, fraud, misleading endorsements or harassment.
6. Failing to disclose materially AI-generated audio, visuals, virtual performers or AI-assisted production in required labels, including falsely listing an AI performer as a verified human artist.
7. Reuploading the same copyrighted content with speed, pitch, tempo, filters, cropping, watermark removal or other edits designed to evade detection.
8. Submitting false ownership assertions, forged licenses, fake permissions, fabricated documents or knowing material misrepresentations in copyright complaints or counter-notices.
9. Selling music or designs that rely on protected material without required synchronization, master, mechanical, sample, trademark, voice or other applicable clearances.
10. Reposting paid, exclusive or subscriber-only media without authorized distribution rights; bypassing technical copy-protection or creator access limits where unlawful.
11. Using misleading product descriptions, stolen product photos, unauthorized branding or counterfeit origin claims.
12. Ignoring an infringement removal or suspension by repeatedly reposting, creating evasion accounts, or routing sales through another account.

## 3. User declarations and platform license
Before commercial upload/sale, creators should explicitly affirm: (a) they have the rights/permissions or valid legal basis for all material; (b) their representations are accurate; (c) AI involvement and virtual identities are disclosed; (d) no misleading impersonation is intended; (e) they accept the applicable terms. PIKK receives only the limited license necessary to host, promote, technically process and deliver content as stated in the Terms; ownership is not transferred merely by upload.

## 4. Reports, notice, review, counter-notification and appeals
Publish a clear rights complaint path for copyright, trademark, counterfeit goods, deceptive identity and voice/likeness complaints. Securely capture complainant identity/contact, right or protected work, exact URL/content ID, authorization/good-faith affirmation, signature for DMCA claims, and supporting evidence. Triage urgent fraud/impersonation, preserve evidence, restrict content where warranted, notify uploader when appropriate, allow a meaningful response and appeals. For DMCA-compliant claims, act expeditiously; follow the statutory counter-notice/reinstatement process and repeat-infringer policy. Avoid publicly exposing complainants' private information or promising perfect detection.

## 5. Proportional enforcement
Possible responses: educational warning, AI-label correction, restricted discovery, temporary hold on sales or payout, content removal, account suspension, repeat-infringer termination, fraud referral or lawfully required disclosure. Serious infringement, identity fraud, counterfeiting or deliberate evasion may warrant immediate action. Case-by-case decisions; user appeals and evidence review. Do not set an arbitrary 'three strikes' rule as a substitute for a legally adequate repeat-infringer termination policy.

## 6. PIKK safeguards that must actually be implemented
- Appoint/register a DMCA agent with the U.S. Copyright Office and publicly list current required contact details.
- Publish enforceable Terms, acceptable-use/IP policy, creator seller agreement, AI disclosure policy, privacy disclosures, copyright notices and counter-notice instructions; log versioned acceptance.
- Reasonably implement repeat-infringer termination policy, appropriate takedown/restore workflows and standard technical measures under 17 USC 512.
- Verify seller/artist ownership before payouts as appropriate; preserve claims, receipts and audit logs with access controls and retention rules.
- Set a lawful notice path for trademark, counterfeits, right of publicity and digital replica cases, since DMCA copyright safe harbor does not cover all such claims.
- Define proportionate content screening and seller moderation without falsely claiming automated scanning is ready.
- Obtain adequate commercial rights for any PIKK-curated or rebroadcast media; platform-wide music/social licenses are not automatically granted by upload.
- Counsel review: representations/warranties, indemnification, limitation of liability, dispute resolution, governing law, venue and consumer rights, tailored to applicable laws. These provisions do not prevent lawsuits or excuse PIKK's own misconduct.

## 7. Status and essential caution
This document and any UI summary are *draft policy guidance*, not legal advice and not proof of deployed notice/appeals/indemnification infrastructure. Do not present DMCA registration, automatic fingerprinting, sanctions, seller validation, AI detection or staff review as active without verification.
